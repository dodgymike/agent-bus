## Description

An independent security audit measured the disconnect at the RAW SOCKET rather than from status codes and found it aimed at the wrong party. Same-agent idempotency-key reuse (same key, different payload) returned 409 WITH `Connection: close` and the socket was genuinely closed. Third-party replay of an accepted signed message returned 403 with NO `Connection: close` and the socket stayed open -- and that disconnect was not merely unwired but UNREACHABLE, because both routes a third party can take are caught by authorization checks that fire before the idempotency layer. Net effect: the bus disconnected the party most likely to be honest and let the party most likely to be hostile keep its connection.

The operator decided (2026-08-08) to NARROW the disconnect to the replay path:
1. ADD the disconnect to the sender-mismatch 403 in `checkSignedMint` (internal/httpapi/messages.go) -- this is invariant 10's replay clause.
2. REMOVE it from the same-agent key-reuse 409 on both `POST /v1/send` (internal/httpapi/messages.go writeHubError) and `POST /v1/enroll` (internal/httpapi/auth.go writeAuthError). Those become reject-and-log.
3. The cross-mint 409 was investigated and deliberately NOT disconnected: `hub.ErrUnknownMint` is raised both when a third party spends another agent's reservation and when the SAME agent re-presents its own already-spent reservation under a fresh key. The `h.mints[{agent,op,key}]` miss carries no information about who the presented message_id was minted for, the hub keeps no message-id -> minting-agent index, and internal/store has no lookup by message id. Disconnecting on the sentinel alone would have repeated the exact mistake being fixed, one level down. Left un-disconnected by explicit operator instruction; a follow-up is filed.

Scope: internal/httpapi/** plus CONTRACTS-HTTP.md. Code-only; no deploy.
