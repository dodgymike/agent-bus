## Description

Raised by the ACK-5 security re-audit, 2026-08-21, as an accepted-and-recorded residual rather than a defect in what shipped.

ACK-5 added a per-upstream OUTBOUND in-flight cap in cmd/agent-bus/ackback.go (enterUpstream, maxConcurrentAckTransitsPerUpstream = maxConcurrentRelayIngestsPerPeer / 2 = 4). Its own doc is explicit that it is a CONCURRENCY bound and not a rate limit: an agent under the cap is never slowed, however fast it acknowledges, exactly as peerAdmission never slows a peer under its share.

The residual: a hostile or compromised agent can sustain a 4-concurrent, rate-UNBOUNDED stream of backward acknowledgement hops at an innocent neighbouring bus. Each hop is a real mutual-TLS request that consumes a slot in that neighbour's 8-slot admission bucket for THIS bus, which that neighbour SHARES with our relay message ingest.

What bounds it today, and why this is P2 rather than higher: it is 1:1 and does not amplify (one inbound POST /v1/ack produces at most one outbound hop); each source bus has its OWN 8-slot bucket at a neighbour, keyed per authenticated peer, so 4 leaves 4 for our message ingest specifically and one bus cannot starve another's; and every hop is refused at the far end by the section 6.2 obligation binding unless this bus genuinely owed that peer a job for that correlation key.

It composes with ACK-5-FU-BUSPATH-SENDER (nothing binds an arriving stored bus path prefix to the authenticated peer), which is what lets a peer influence WHICH neighbour is targeted. Consider the two together when either is scheduled.

Candidate shapes, none free: a token bucket per agent principal in front of POST /v1/ack (ACK-CONTRACT.md section 16 Q3 already asks for a meter in front of the outbox sweep and would be the natural home); or a rate as well as a concurrency bound on the outbound side; or accept it explicitly and record the acceptance in DECISIONS.md so the next reader does not rediscover it as a finding.
